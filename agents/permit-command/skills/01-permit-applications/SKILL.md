# Permit Applications

## Purpose
Prepare and submit construction permit applications across all jurisdictions where M4 Development Holdings LLC operates. Manage the permit application lifecycle from preparation through issuance for all entity projects in NC, SC, FL, and AL.

## Applicable Entities
- **M4 General Contractors** - Commercial building permits
- **Overland Grading & Sitework** - Grading, erosion control, and utility permits
- **Southern View Homes** - Residential building permits per lot

## Common Permit Types
- Building Permit (commercial and residential)
- Grading/Land Disturbance Permit
- Erosion and Sediment Control Permit
- Utility Connection Permits (water, sewer, storm)
- Driveway/Encroachment Permit (DOT)
- Mechanical, Electrical, and Plumbing Permits
- Fire Alarm and Sprinkler Permits
- Sign Permits
- Certificate of Occupancy (final permit)

## Permit Application Workflow

### Step 1: Jurisdiction Research
- Identify the Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ) for the project location.
- Research the specific permit requirements, application forms, and submittal checklists for that jurisdiction.
- Determine applicable building codes: NC uses NC Building Code, SC uses International Building Code, FL uses Florida Building Code, AL uses International Building Code with state amendments.
- Check for any special overlay district requirements, historic district reviews, or HOA approvals needed.
- Confirm zoning compliance and whether any variances or special use permits are required.

### Step 2: Application Package Preparation
- Complete the jurisdiction's permit application form with all required fields.
- Assemble the submittal package per the jurisdiction's checklist, typically including:
  - Signed and sealed construction drawings (architectural, structural, MEP, civil).
  - Site plan showing setbacks, parking, and impervious coverage calculations.
  - Structural calculations and energy compliance documentation.
  - Soils/geotechnical report.
  - NPDES erosion control plan (for land disturbance over 1 acre).
  - Contractor license documentation for the applicable entity.
  - Proof of workers' compensation and liability insurance.
- Calculate permit fees based on the jurisdiction's fee schedule and project valuation.

### Step 3: Submission
- Submit the permit application through the jurisdiction's preferred method (online portal, in-person, or mail).
- Pay permit fees and retain fee receipts.
- Log the submission in the permit tracking spreadsheet: project, jurisdiction, permit type, submission date, fee paid, and expected review timeline.
- Set calendar reminders for expected review completion dates.

### Step 4: Plan Review and Response
- Monitor the plan review status and respond to reviewer comments within 5 business days.
- Coordinate with the design team (architect, engineer) to address technical review comments.
- Resubmit corrected documents promptly to avoid resetting the review queue.
- Track resubmission cycles; escalate to entity leadership if a permit is in its third review cycle.

### Step 5: Permit Issuance and Distribution
- Upon permit approval, download or pick up the issued permit.
- Distribute the permit to: Project Manager, Superintendent (for field posting), and the project file.
- Upload the issued permit to the project's 03 - Permits & Approvals folder on Google Drive.
- Post the building permit at the job site per jurisdictional requirements.
- Update the project schedule with the actual permit issuance date.

## Jurisdiction-Specific Notes
- **NC municipalities** often require separate zoning and building permit reviews. Stormwater permits may go through a different department.
- **SC (especially coastal counties)**: May require OCRM (Ocean and Coastal Resource Management) permits for projects near the coast.
- **FL**: Require NOC (Notice of Commencement) to be recorded after permit issuance. Private provider inspections available as an alternative.
- **AL**: Some rural jurisdictions have no local building department; permits may be through the county or state fire marshal.
